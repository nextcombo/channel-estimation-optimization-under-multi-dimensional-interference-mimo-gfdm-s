✿MIMO Non-Orthogonal Multicarrier Systems Based on Guidance and Cyclic Prefix (CP) for Channel Estimation ✿Abstract Driven by the demands of 5G applications, the limitations of Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) are becoming increasingly prominent, making non-orthogonal multicarrier technology a subject of extensive research. Unlike OFDM that utilizes orthogonal pilot signals to observe the channel, when multiple input multiple output (MIMO) becomes part of the transmission, the pilots in non-orthogonal waveforms are affected by interference from symbols, carriers, and antennas. The introduction of cyclic prefixes (CP) into multicarrier systems not only protects the signal from symbol interference but also allows for cyclic interpretation of the channel, simplifying estimation and equalization techniques. However, receivers typically discard the CP information. This paper addresses the issue of multidimensional interference in non-orthogonal waveforms by proposing a parallel interference cancellation (PIC) method based on MIMO linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE), for joint channel estimation and equalization in non-orthogonal waveforms. In contrast to conventional approaches, this paper strategically locates the pilots in the time domain to fully leverage the pilot information within the CP. The proposed algorithm is applied to Generalized Frequency Division Multiplexing (GFDM), a flexible non-orthogonal waveform, leveraging the block cyclic property of GFDM to analyze the complexity of the CP-aided LMMSE-PIC channel estimation. Simulation results show that using GFDM pilot CP information can improve frame error rate (FER) performance by up to 2.4dB compared to OFDM signals.  
Introduction The vertical market for future wireless communication systems is expected to accommodate various application types, demanding not only higher data rates but also ultra-reliable, low-latency communications (URLLC), asynchronous multi-access, and heterogeneous network architectures. Research has shown that OFDM's flexibility is insufficient to meet diverse application needs, thus increasing academic and industrial efforts in exploring alternative waveforms. Despite this, OFDM remains selected as a technical solution for early versions of 5G standardization due to its suitability for non-orthogonal waveforms, which still require further testing and hardware-in-the-loop experiments.
The MIMO transmission is a core component of future wireless communication. Compared to the simple and low-complexity implementation of OFDM, the design of the receiver end for MIMO non-orthogonal multicarrier systems is more challenging—the receiver needs to deal with multiple interferences such as Intersymbol Interference (ISI), InterCarrier Interference (ICI), and InterArray Interference (IAI). This multidimensional interference affects the reference signal received (i.e., pilot), posing challenges to channel estimation (CE) and subsequent signal equalization (EQ), especially in broadband communication scenarios where the channel is dual selective. On the other hand, the good locality of the pilot in both time and frequency domains in non-orthogonal waveforms allows for the utilization of the pilot energy within cyclic prefix (CP).
Existing research has explored the idea of enhancing channel estimation by utilizing CP information through Kalman filters, but these methods are only applicable to orthogonal waveforms. This paper leverages the symbol-time locality in non-orthogonal waveforms to enhance channel estimation performance by reusing the pilot energy within CP—if the pilot energy is properly positioned at the end of the block, it will still maintain high energy when replicated into CP. Unlike existing research, the proposed method is applicable to all linear modulations subject to interference constraints and their MIMO applications.
The conventional approach to data symbol detection in non-orthogonal multicarrier systems is to use independent channel estimation and equalization modules. Although there are various joint channel estimation and equalization algorithms for MIMO-OFDM in existing literature, they cannot be directly applied to non-orthogonal multicarrier systems because these algorithms do not consider ISI, ICI, and IAI. The authors' previous study, based on the assumption that ISI and ICI introduce additional correlated noise on white noise, derived minimum mean square (LS) and Least Mean Square Error (LMMSE) estimators for non-orthogonal waveforms. However, these non-iterative methods do not achieve optimal channel estimation performance, resulting in significant error floors in the high SNR region. To the authors' knowledge, no existing literature provides a channel estimation scheme that can be directly applied to MIMO interference-limited non-orthogonal waveforms while utilizing its CP information.
The contributions of this paper are threefold:
1. Based on preliminary research, the widely accepted LMMSE parallel interference cancellation (PIC) method for MIMO non-orthogonal multicarrier is derived, which is used for near-optimal joint channel estimation and equalization in MIMO systems. The derivation process fully considers the pilot information of the entire block, including CP, and introduces a new technique to simplify equivalent channel models. For GFDM waveforms and their block cyclic structure, an Sequential LMMSE (SLMMSE) channel estimator with low complexity is derived.
2. Through statistical floating-point operations (FLOPs), the complexity of each algorithm is analyzed in detail, providing a basis for balancing algorithms under different application scenarios (e.g., URLLC prefers lower complexity transmission, while video streaming applications have lower complexity requirements).
3. Through extensive simulations, the performance of each algorithm is evaluated under various scenarios. By combining modulation and coding schemes (MCS), the algorithms that are more optimal in terms of complexity or robustness are identified, and comparisons are made with MIMO-OFDM transmission as the benchmark.
Methodology:
1. System Model and Fundamental Theory
The linear modulation model with CP is first defined, clarifying the way of combining pilot and data symbols, as well as the impact of CP insertion on pilot energy; assuming multipath Rayleigh fading MIMO channels, the channel response and covariance matrix in both time and frequency domains are modeled, laying the foundation for subsequent algorithm derivations.
2. CP-Aided Channel Estimation
In the single input single output (SISO) scenario: considering the block fading characteristics of two consecutive transmission blocks, incorporating the impact of ISI leakage from the previous block on the current block's CP, by defining a window matrix to focus on the region of pilot energy, the non-diagonal Toeplitz channel matrix is approximated to a cycle matrix to simplify computation, leading to the derivation of CP-aided LMMSE channel estimation formulas.
3. MIMO Extension:
The method from the SISO scenario is extended to multi-antenna systems, introducing block diagonalized window matrices, taking into account multi-antenna interference, and deriving the MIMO scenario's LMMSE estimator and the calculation method of the noise-interference covariance matrix.
3. Block Diagonalization of GFDM and Ordered LMMSE Estimation for GFDM Waveforms: In response to the block cyclic characteristics of GFDM waveforms, effective channel block diagonalization is achieved through Discrete ZAK Transform (DZT), which decomposes the received signal into multiple short sequences to reduce complexity; Propose the LMMSE (SLMMSE) estimation method, which uses the time stability of channel statistical characteristics to update the channel estimation values recursively and gradually integrate new observation information, thereby improving the estimation accuracy.
4. Low-Complexity CP Aided Channel Estimation: By utilizing the pilot energy in CP, adjust the window matrix to select observation samples containing CP. Based on the block diagonalization characteristic of GFDM, reuse the estimation results from SLMMSE as initial values to further optimize channel estimation performance, achieving performance improvement while slightly increasing complexity.
5. To address the issue of estimation errors caused by interference from data symbols in high SNR regions, a two-step iterative method is proposed: firstly, estimate data symbols using a component-wise conditional unbiased (CWCU) LMMSE equalizer, and then use the estimated results to eliminate interference in pilot observations in parallel, update the noise-interference covariance matrix of the channel estimator, and iteratively improve the estimation and equalization performance.
Conclusion This paper proposes a pilot and CP-aided joint channel estimation and equalization technique suitable for MIMO non-orthogonal waveforms, with a focus on the analysis of algorithm complexity issues in the context of interfered-limited MIMO GFDM systems. Simulation results show that the low-complexity SLMMSE and CP-SLMMSE algorithms can achieve performance comparable to high-complexity algorithms under robust modulation coding schemes (such as QPSK 1/3); however, ignoring interference terms leads to significant performance degradation when dealing with higher order modulation coding schemes, necessitating the introduction of PIC mechanisms.
The CP-LMMSE-PIC algorithm achieves optimal frame error rate (FER) performance at a higher complexity, with a significant gain in signal-to-noise ratio compared to OFDM systems. Based on the analysis of FER performance and complexity, this paper provides recommendations for algorithm selection: whether or not there is mobility, CP-SLMMSE is suitable for robust modulation encoding schemes (QPSK 1/3), CP-LMMSE is suitable for moderate modulation encoding schemes (16-QAM 2/3), while CP-LMMSE-PIC is the optimal choice for high-rate transmission (64-QAM 3/4).
